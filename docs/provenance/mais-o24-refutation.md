The three conclusions, on the subclass `𝕄(sk,λ,μ)` cut by a supplied list:

- **(a)** `𝚫_M = 𝚫_{M'}` implies `M = M'`;
- **(b)** a quantitative recovery modulus below an explicit threshold;
- **(c)** for each fixed graph `G` and Lebesgue-almost-every `u`,

## The three steps, as checked

**1. The collision is an open box, not a point.**
`Model.jointProb_congr_at` is the general fact behind it, added to
`Causal/Model.lean` for this: `factor` reads `cpt` only at the assignment it
scores, so two models agreeing on the tables at **one** assignment give that
assignment the same probability under *every* intervention profile — at
different graphs included. Since `g` is supported at `(1,1)`, agreement there is
the whole transform (`Δ_eq_half_sub_joint_real`). The `X → Y` model at `(p,q₀,q₁)`
and the edgeless model at `(p,q₁)` agree at `(1,1)` by construction: the source
row `q₀` is never read, because the sum conditions on `X = 1`. `behaviorEq_box`
states it. Both models are in the printed margin class throughout
`o24Box = (0.45,0.55) × (0.15,0.25) × (0.75,0.85)`
(`marginClass_arrowModel`, `marginClass_edgeModel`); (M2), (M3) and (M6) read the
gap alone and come from MAIS-O23's witness, (M5) holds of every model on this
skeleton because `𝐙 = 𝐂`, and (M1) and (M4) are what the box was chosen to give
— the edge is separated by more than `½`.

**2. Conclusion (a) forces a polynomial to vanish on the box.**
`exists_o24Value_eq_zero`: at a box point where no supplied polynomial vanishes,
`exists_pos_le_of_forall_pos` produces a `μ` below every one of the finitely many
values, both models land in `𝕄(sk,λ,μ)`, and (a) identifies two models carrying
different graphs. So the box is covered by the vanishing loci of one finite list
— the `X → Y` list, plus the edgeless list renamed along `edgeIndex`, the
injection sending each edgeless coordinate to the `X = 1` row.
`Analysis.volume_setOf_exists_eval_eq_zero` (new, domain-neutral) says a finite
list of **nonzero** polynomials has a null vanishing locus, and the box has
positive volume. Hence `exists_spec_eq_zero`: some supplied polynomial is
identically zero once the utility is frozen at the collision's.

**3. Conclusion (c) fails beside the collision utility.**
The vanishing is at `u₀`, and (c) is asserted only almost everywhere, so `u₀`
may be exactly what (c) discards. `not_o24ExcludedSetSmall_of_spec_eq_zero`
moves it: fix a compact table box `K ⊆ chartBox G`, note `Leb(K) > 0`, and use
`generalized_tube_lemma` on the continuous map `(θ,u) ↦ Q(θ,u)` to get a
neighbourhood of `u₀` on which `|Q| < μ` uniformly over `K`. Intersect it with
the interior of (c)'s own utility box — `u₀` is interior, its coordinates being
`1/4` and `3/4` — and the result is open and nonempty, hence of positive measure,
hence meets the full-measure set on which (c) is asserted. At such a `u`, all of
`K` is inside (c)'s excluded set, so `Leb(K) ≤ S^a μ^b`.

## The one repair

**The note's last step is circular as written.** It chooses utilities `uₙ → u₀`
from (c)'s full-measure set *first*, sets `sₙ = sup_K |Q(·,uₙ)|`, and only then
builds `μₙ = max{2sₙ, 1/n}`. But `prob:effective` quantifies `∀ μ` **outside**
the three conclusions, so (c)'s almost-every null set is free to depend on `μ` —
including on the `μₙ` the choice of `uₙ` just produced. The set the `uₙ` were
drawn from is not the set that must contain them.

`O24ExcludedSetSmall` keeps print's quantifier order, which is why the gap is
visible in the formalization rather than absorbed by it.

**The repair is to reverse the two choices, and it costs nothing.** `μ` here is
fixed from `S`, `a`, `b` and `Leb(K)` alone — none of which mention a utility —
and the utility is chosen afterwards. Concretely `μ = min(¼, c^{1/b})` with
`c = Leb(K)/(2·S^a)`, so `S^a μ^b ≤ Leb(K)/2 < Leb(K)`, and then step 3 finds a
`u`. No countable union, no strengthening of (c), no reading print does not
support.

An earlier draft of this file proposed a countable-`μ` family and a union of
countably many null sets. That works too, and is unnecessary.

## Two smaller notes

- **The empty list.** The note writes `A_G = ∏_j Q^G_j` with the empty product
  as `1`, then argues one factor vanishes identically. With both lists empty
  that step reads `1 = 0`. It is not a gap in the mathematics — an empty
  certificate cuts nothing and (a) already fails, which
  `Examples.Causal.EffectiveGenericity.not_o24Identifies_nil` has recorded since
  before this work — but a formalization has to case-split, and the version here
  avoids the product entirely by covering the box with the list itself.
- **The obstruction is sharper than the bundle.**
  `not_o24_identifies_and_excluded` takes conclusions (a) and (c) and nothing
  else, so it survives dropping conclusion (b), the polynomial size bounds, and
  the construction-time clause. `O24Solution` carries all of them and is empty a
  fortiori. This matches the note's own scope paragraph.

## What it costs the agenda

`Examples/Causal/EffectiveGenericity.lean` distinguishes two reasons
`O24Solution` might have no inhabitant: the acceptable one, that
`prob:effective` is open, and the unacceptable one, that its fields are jointly
contradictory — which would make every downstream *"for every solution to
MAIS-O24, …"* vacuously true. **It is the second.**

The downstream statement is MAIS-O26 (`CONJ-003`), whose `Prop` opens
`∀ sol : O24Solution`. `Examples.Conjectures.MAIS.maisO26_exactRate_holds` now
proves it — vacuously. `Conjectures/MAIS/O26.lean` had already flagged that
`maisO26_exactRate` "may instead be vacuously true"; that question is closed, in
the unwelcome direction. The empty-class refutation route recorded on `CONJ-003`
can never be run, because its antecedent needs an `O24Solution`.

A MAIS-O26 that says something would have to be stated over a class that does
not come from a solution to `prob:effective`. A MAIS-O24 that has one would have
to weaken (c) — letting the excluded-volume constant depend on `u`, imposing a
margin from the utility discriminant, or measuring exclusion jointly in `(θ,u)`,
which are the three repairs the note itself proposes.
