# MAIS-O33 refuted: no positive corruption fraction is uniformly tolerable

`prob:corruption` (MAIS-A2 Problem 4.12 at the pin; see
[`mais-source-pin.md`](mais-source-pin.md)) asks *"Determine
`η* := sup{η : η uniformly tolerable}`. Is `η* > 0`?"* The answer is **no**, and
since 2026-08-30 it is machine-checked:

| no restricted class of algorithms does better | `AISafetyAtlas.Examples.Conjectures.MAIS.not_uniformlyTolerableWithin` |
| `η* = 0` given print's own baseline, with no empty-supremum convention | `AISafetyAtlas.Examples.Conjectures.MAIS.maisO33_etaStarIsZeroGivenBaseline_holds` |

| the witness | `AISafetyAtlas.Examples.Causal.O33Corruption.exists_not_tolerantAt` |

## What was checked: print's statement, and the candidate's claim

These are two different things and the row keeps them apart.

**The `Prop` transcribes print**, `prob:corruption` in the pinned agenda, which
is `CONJ-023`'s `source_ref`. It is not a transcription of the issue.

**The candidate's proof was not verified line by line.** MAIS issue
[#9](https://github.com/lionellevine/MAIS/issues/9) — Svyatoslav Novikov
(kumino), generated by OpenAI Codex, 2026-08-04, CC BY 4.0, no human referee
review claimed — proposed the answer `η* = 0` and an argument for it, and
[`mais-o33-statability.md`](mais-o33-statability.md) audits that argument by
reading and finds no error. What is machine-checked here is the **claim**: the
Lean proof follows the candidate's strategy — two indistinguishable worlds, one
common corruption, a counting bound on the goals that carry no immediate win —
but runs it at a *different instance* (`δ = 1/2` rather than `0`,
action-independent kernels rather than one skewed row), so several of the
candidate's own steps have no counterpart in the Lean. A reader who wants "issue
#9's proof is correct" will not find it here; what is here is "issue #9's answer
is correct, and here is a proof."

The attachment is pinned at its own frozen revision, which agrees with the pin on
`prob:corruption` byte for byte; see

## The argument, in three steps

**One instance.** `𝐒 = Fin (m+2)`, `𝐀 = Fin 2`, `n = 101`, `δ = 1/2`. Then
`|𝐀| ≥ 2`, `n > 1` and `(n−1)(1−δ) = 50 > 4`, so this is one of the instances
print quantifies over — and one is enough, because *uniform* tolerability is a
claim about all of them at once. The target radius is `2/√50 ≈ 0.283`; the two
environments are `4/5` apart, and `4/5 > 2·(2/√50) ≈ 0.566`
(`O33Corruption.o33_radius`).

**Two agents that almost agree.** `Causal.exists_isDeltaBounded_prescribing`
builds, in each world, a `(δ,n)`-bounded goal-conditioned agent that opens with
action `0` at every (start state, goal) pair whose goal carries a depth-one
*Now* disjunct that `(s, 0)` already satisfies, and near-optimally elsewhere. The
first clause costs nothing: such a goal is achieved with probability `1` by
playing `0`, in **every** environment, because a *Now* condition reads `(s₀,a₀)`
and never a transition probability. So the two agents differ only on the goals
carrying no such disjunct, which `Causal.card_corruptionDomain_avoiding` counts
and `Causal.exceptional_ratio_lt` bounds by `2^{1−R}` with `R = 2^{2|𝐒|−1}` —
doubly exponentially small in the state count, so raising `m` clears any
positive `η`.

**One oracle, two worlds.** `ρ` is taken to be the second agent's honest
first-action map. It is a `0`-corruption there and an `η`-corruption of the
first (`Causal.isCorruption_of_agree_off_avoiding`), so the analyst faces the
*same function* in both worlds, its one output law would have to put `2/3` on
each of two disjoint reconstruction balls, and
`Causal.not_tolerantAt_of_common_corruption` closes it.

**No query bound is used.** The refutation holds at every budget, which is why
print's polynomial plays no role: this is indistinguishability, not query
complexity. That is also the shape of print's own first gloss — *"since `|𝚿_n|`
is astronomically larger than any polynomial budget, a fraction-`η` corruption
can cover every query"*. Here it covers more than the queries: it covers every
pair at which the two worlds could have differed.

## Two changes of instance, and what each bought

Both are admissible instances of `prob:corruption`, and each removes a
dependency the candidate's own choice would have forced.

**`δ = 1/2` instead of the note's `δ = 0`.** At `δ = 0` the boundedness clause
reads `P(τ ⊨ ψ | π, s₀) = ⨆_{π'} …`, so the two agents would have to be exactly
optimal at every pair, and the existence of optimal policies for reachability
objectives on a finite controlled Markov process becomes unavoidable. Mathlib
does not carry it. At any `δ > 0` ordinary supremum approximation suffices
(`Causal.exists_achieveProb_ge`), and `δ = 1/2` still leaves `(n−1)(1−δ) = 50`.

**Action-independent kernels instead of the note's one skewed row.** The two
environments put mass `9/10` and `1/10` on state `0` and spread the rest
uniformly, whatever the action — print's own action-independent class, the one
its myopic converse is stated over. Then `Causal.trajectoryLaw_congr` makes the
trajectory law *the same measure* for every policy, so the near-optimal choices
can be made per (start state, goal) and spliced without a congruence lemma for
`ProbabilityTheory.Kernel.traj` that Mathlib does not state. The predecessor
audit recorded that lemma as the one thing standing between the layer and this
theorem; choosing the instance better removed the need for it.

This is not a weakening. `thm:rabe` claims a `(δ,n)`-bounded agent determines
the kernel to within `√(2/50) ≈ 0.2` on *any* communicating instance, these two
are `0.8` apart, and the agents built here agree except on the exceptional
goals. So the informative pairs really are that doubly exponentially small set —
which is the content of `prob:corruption`, not an artefact of the class.

## What is checked and what is not

**Checked.** Print's statement, at print's quantifiers: all finite instances with
`|𝐀| ≥ 2`, `n > 1`, `(n−1)(1−δ) > 4`; every communicating environment; every
`(δ,n)`-bounded goal-conditioned agent at print's trajectory-prefix history
type; every `η`-corruption; one randomized adaptive algorithm and one polynomial
budget across all instances. Every declaration is axiom-clean — `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` — and there is no `sorry`.

**The history axis is closed by proof, not by prose.** Reading print's history as
"states only" is *not* a safe simplification, and the direction argument for it
fails: `(δ,n)`-boundedness compares against `max_{π'}` over the same class, so
narrowing the class lowers the threshold too and a policy near-optimal among
state policies need not be near-optimal among trajectory-prefix ones. `Causal.inducedPolicy` closes it — the action a trajectory-prefix policy
takes is a function of the states, computed by running the same rule at earlier
times — and `Causal.optimalProbFull_eq` shows the two suprema coincide.

**Three readings remain, all recorded on `CONJ-023`.** Print's `max` is read as
`⨆` (which selects a sub-collection of `A(E,n,δ)`, so the refutation is the
stronger claim); the empty disjunction is excluded from `𝚿_n`, which print's
own depth clause settles, since the empty set has no maximum; and the corruption
map is carried as a total function, which nothing observes because queries range
over `𝐒 × 𝚿_n`.

**The goal event is measurable, and that is now proved rather than asserted.**
`Causal.achieveProb` applies the trajectory law to
`{ω | CompositeSatisfies Ψ …}`, and `Causal.measurableSet_compositeSatisfies`
shows that set measurable, so the value is print's `P(τ ⊨ ψ | π, s₀)` and not
merely the measure's canonical extension to an arbitrary set. The proof follows
print's semantics: a sub-goal reads one state–action pair, that pair is a
function of the states up to its time because the policy is deterministic, *Now*
and *Next* fix the time, *Eventually* is a countable union over it with a finite
minimality condition, a sequential goal is a countable union over its head's
achievement time, and a composite goal is a finite union over its disjuncts. No
step of the refutation needs it — every use of `achieveProb` is monotone or an
almost-everywhere congruence, both valid for the extension — but without it the
row would be transcribing a number that is only *bounded by* print's.

**What print's *"one randomized algorithm"* means, and why `UniformAnalyst` is
it.** The word is not defined in `prob:corruption`, so it is read from the
section that sets up every query problem in the agenda, `subsec:queries`. That
section defines an analyst as something that *"adaptively issues queries
`t = 1,…,N`"* and defines the figure of merit as *"the infimum over (randomized)
analyst **strategies**"*. `N` bounds queries, not steps. A2 contains no
computational model anywhere — no machine, no circuit, no time or space bound,
no complexity class — and its one lower-bound argument (`rem:packing`) is Fano
and Yao, which are information-theoretic and indifferent to computability. In
this document *algorithm* denotes a randomized adaptive query strategy, and that
is exactly `Conjectures.MAIS.UniformAnalyst`: one strategy, one estimator and one
polynomial, all chosen before the instance, which is the quantifier order the
word *single* fixes. It withholds nothing from them: print's instance is
`(𝐒, 𝐀, n, δ)` and all four reach the strategy and the estimator. Only the query
*bound* is `δ`-free — print writes `p(|𝐒|,|𝐀|,n)` — which is why `coeff` and
`deg` do not see it. Withholding `δ` from the protocol would narrow the analyst
class and block the transfer to print, even though the witness defeats every
strategy at the fixed `δ` it uses.

**And if a reader insists the word means *computable*, that reading is refuted
too.** On that reading `UniformlyTolerable` would quantify over a class wider
than print's, since a Lean function is not a computable one — so the atlas does
not rest on its own reading. `UniformlyTolerableWithin C` restricts the analyst
by an **arbitrary** predicate `C` — computability, finite describability,
anything the word could carry — and `not_uniformlyTolerableWithin` refutes every
such reading at once. The reason is structural rather than lucky: the witness
defeats *every* strategy at one instance, so narrowing the class cannot rescue
tolerability. What is not formalized on that reading is a *class-relative*
threshold `η*_C`, and nothing here needs one: the statement that carries the
answer is *"no positive `η` is tolerable"*, and that is proved for every `C`. A
class-relative supremum would repeat the empty-set question one level down — if
`C` admits no analyst meeting print's baseline, `η*_C` is a supremum of a
possibly empty set — which is exactly the distinction the next paragraph keeps.

**`η* = 0` carries a caveat that `η* > 0` does not, and the caveat is now a
hypothesis rather than a footnote.** That no positive `η` is tolerable is proved.
That `η = 0` *is* tolerable is `thm:rabe`, which print cites and this repository
does not formalize, so the `≥ 0` half of `etaStar_eq_zero` is carried by
`Real.sSup ∅ = 0` rather than by an inhabitant. Rather than leave that in prose,
`Conjectures.MAIS.maisO33_baselineTolerable` names print's baseline as a `Prop`
and `maisO33_etaStarIsZeroGivenBaseline_holds` proves the same equation from it,
with `0 ≤ η*` coming from `le_csSup` at an inhabitant. So the ledger's
determine-clause is certified twice: once unconditionally for this module's
totalized `etaStar`, and once conditionally in a form that does not use the
empty-supremum convention at all. `not_maisO33_etaStarPos` remains the clause
that depends on neither, and it is print's actual question.

**What closing the baseline would take, precisely.** It is more than porting a
published theorem. `thm:rabe` says *"`π` alone **determines** an estimate"* with
an error bound; it names no analyst, no query count, and no success probability,
and A2 carries no proof of it — it is cited to Richens–Abel–Bellot–Everitt 2025.
The algorithm and its budget live in the **unnumbered paragraph** after the
theorem: either-or goals *"first action `a` commits you to achieving this
transition at most `k` times in `n` tries"*, whose answer locates a binomial
median, swept over `k`, *"about `|𝐒|²|𝐀|n` queries in all"*. So
`UniformlyTolerable 0` needs that construction, a proof that a `(δ,n)`-bounded
agent's first action really locates the median, the passage from medians to
transition probabilities, and the packaging as one analyst uniform in the
instance.

The accuracy is not where the difficulty is. `thm:rabe`'s bound is
`√(2P(1−P)/((n−1)(1−δ)))`, and `P(1−P) ≤ ¼` puts it under
`1/√(2(n−1)(1−δ))`, against `prob:corruption`'s target of
`2/√((n−1)(1−δ))` — a factor of about `2.8` of slack, which is also what A2's
own `ε* ≤ √(2/((n−1)(1−δ)))` says. The work is mechanizing the median sweep.
